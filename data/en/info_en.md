# Child Development - Notes

Child development: first to third month
During these months, the growth of the child's height and weight is very fast. The growth process of the child in these months is similar to the rapid growth that he had during the first month. In this month, the child will grow between 2.5 and 3.8 cm in height and around 907 grams in weight. These numbers only represent the expected average, and it is possible that your child's growth will be slower or faster, or that his growth will suddenly increase at some point. If your baby is fussy or wants more food than usual at 6 weeks, it's probably because of his growth spurt. If the child's restlessness lasts more than a day or two, discuss the matter with the doctor. At each visit with your doctor, he will measure your child's height, weight, and head circumference and compare these numbers with the standard child growth chart (there are different charts for boys and girls).

Child growth: 4 to 7 months
Children at this age continue to grow rapidly, growing in body size, physical skills, and the ability to interact with the world around them. Many of the new skills they learn can be used to help them eat more solid foods. In fact, at this time or in a few months, your baby will be able to taste food other than breast milk or formula for the first time. Of course, the main food of the baby is still milk, but he can discover many new tastes little by little. As long as your baby is still growing, his possibly strange eating habits should not worry you. During these three months, the weight of most children doubles their birth weight. During these months, the child gains about 560 grams of weight and 2 cm is added to his height.

8 to 12 month old child growth
The growth of the child in the first year of his life is very fast and his weight reaches twice or more than twice his initial weight. After passing the one-year mark, the growth process slows down a bit. During this one year, the growth process of your child's height, weight and head circumference has been recorded by the doctor, and you can review your child's growth chart during this time and ask your doctor any questions you have in this regard. When looking at your child's growth chart, compare his or her growth to yourself and not to other children. Every baby has its own size and physical characteristics, but as long as your baby continues to grow, there is nothing to worry about.

Child development: one to two years old
By the middle of this time, most children can walk and begin to learn to talk. At the birth of two years, the child's face no longer looks like a baby, and at this time he is already a minor. As children grow and their physical abilities increase, they will have more physical activity. During the second year, the child's growth rate decreases compared to before. In this year, the child will probably gain about 2.27 kg in weight and will grow in height by about 10 to 12 cm. After two years of this time, the child reaches about half of his adult height and his head size reaches 90% of his adult head size. Boys grow more in weight than girls, but their height growth is almost the same. At this time, the thing that attracts your attention more than your child's physical growth is the change in his appearance and face. The child's body parts begin to change. Instead of a bulging belly and short arms and legs, which are suitable for crawling on the ground and walking on all fours, the child will gradually find a stretched and thinner body, and because of the high activity, his body will become more muscular. Your baby's face at this time looks more like a preschooler than a baby.

Child development: 2 to 3 years old
During the third year of life, most toddlers gain about 1.8 kg in weight and between 5 and 8 cm in height. They are extremely active and train in many physical ways. They run around and learn new skills every day, like shooting a ball or riding a tricycle. A child's appetite at this time can be very different, which is normal. Also, your child may only show interest in eating a certain food. If you refuse to give this particular food to the child, this habit will soon fall from his head. In order for your child to develop healthy eating habits, try to give him a variety of different foods and let him decide which food to eat and how much of it. Each child is genetically different in size and height, but a healthy toddler should generally continue to grow in a proportionate manner. In each visit to the doctor, the child's height and weight are measured and recorded in a table. This will allow the doctor to monitor your child's development and if any abnormal process occurs, he will be able to diagnose and investigate it.

Child development: from 4 to 5 years old
Children at this age still have high physical activity, but their learning is more focused and their excitement during learning is less. Children at this age gain about 2 kg in weight and about 5 to 8 cm in height every year. An average 4-year-old child weighs 40 pounds and is 40 inches tall. Preschoolers are still learning and improving their gross and gross motor skills (such as moving their arms and legs and playing) as well as their finer motor skills (such as drawing, making crafts, and solving puzzles). Children at this age can usually stand on one leg and crawl. Children's play at this age becomes very creative and imaginative, which plays a very important role in the child's mental development. Therefore, you should pay attention that your child has enough time to play creative games (either alone or with his friends). He may be doing anything at this time, including drawing, living in a fantasy house, or role-playing. Although each child has its own size and shape, in general, all children should grow in a certain way. In order to monitor the physical development of the child, it is necessary that you have regular visits to the doctor. In these sessions, the doctor will measure the weight and height of the child and draw these results in a chart, and then compare your child's growth with the average growth of other children of the same age and gender.

Help your child grow
Having normal growth is one of the most important and main signs of a child's health, which is achieved by eating well, sleeping a lot and exercising regularly. But in general, your child's growth pattern depends to a large extent on his genetics. If you overfeed your child or give him more than the prescribed amount of vitamins and minerals, this will not make your child grow taller. Eating too much food can make a child obese. Preschoolers may be picky eaters (eating too many small meals) but it's still important to give them the variety of foods they need. In addition to having good nutrition, it is necessary for the child to have at least one hour of physical activity a day. Children at this age are naturally active, so don't stop them and encourage them to be active, and the only thing you need to do is to provide a safe environment for the child's activity and exploration.

توصیه‌هایی برای والدین
خواسته کودک خود را برآورده کنید و در کنار او باشید: این موضوع خصوصاً زمانی اهمیت دارد که کودک شما مریض، گرسنه یا ناراحت است و یا به آرامش نیاز دارد. کودکان به شیوه‌های مثبت نیز نشان می‌دهند که به شما نیاز دارند مانند گفتن کلمات بی‌معنی (مشابه تکلم)، صدا درآوردن و خندیدن. اگر شما به این حرکات کودک به شیوه‌ای محبت‌آمیز و مثبت پاسخ دهید، به رشد مغزی کودک خود کمک کرده‌اید.
خانه‌ای امن و محبت‌آمیز برای کودک خود فراهم کنید: محیطی برای کودک خود ایجاد کنید که کودکتان بتواند روی آن حساب کند در آن احساس امنیت کند. سعی کنید فضای خانه را آرام نگه دارید.
به کودک خود کمک کنید محیط داخل و بیرون از خانه را کشف کند. بازی کردن به کودکان کمک می‌کند که یاد بگیرند و شما اولین همبازی کودک خود هستید. انجام دادن بازی‌های بسیار ساده به کودک کمک می‌کند که محیط و افراد پیرامون خود را بشناسد. به خاطر داشته باشید که در طول روز حتماً با کودک خود صحبت کنید. به کودک خود بگویید که در حال حاضر چه اتفاقی در اطراف او دارد رخ می‌دهد و به هر چیز جالبی که در اطراف خود می‌بینید اشاره کنید و به او کمک کنید که حواس دیگر خود مانند شنیدن، لمس کردن، مزه کردن و بو کردن را نیز به کار گیرد.
کودک خود را مرتباً برای معاینه و چکاپ نزد پزشک ببرید. کودک شما باید به صورت منظم توسط یک پزشک معاینه شود. واکسیناسیون کودک را به موقع انجام دهید. در مورد روند رشد کودک خود به پزشک توضیح دهید و از او بخواهید به شما بگوید که در این مرحله از رشد کودک باید انتظار چه چیزی را داشته باشید.
با گروه‌ها و انجمن‌های کودک ارتباط برقرار کنید. سعی کنید بفهمید که چه خدمات، انجمن‌ها، جلسات یا گروه‌هایی در نزدیکی شما قرار دارند که در رابطه با رشد کودک فعالیت دارند. در بسیاری از کانون‌ها، خدماتی برای خانواده‌های جوان وجود دارد. انجمن‌ها و جلسات مشاوره گروهی خانواده بهترین مکانی است ک در آن می‌توانید با متخصصین رشد کودک در ارتباط باشد و سؤالات خود را زا آن‌ها بپرسید و همچنین با پدران و مادران دیگر آشنا شوید. اگر نمی‌دانید از کجا شروع کنید سعی کنید با نزدیک‌ترین کانون فرهنگی، فرهنگسرا یا کتابخانه‌ی نزدیک خود تماس بگیرید و دراین‌باره از آن‌ها سؤال کنید.
بهترین مرکز نگهداری کودک را برای فرزند خود انتخاب کنید. اگر به هر دلیلی نمی‌توانید در کنار کودک خود باشید، او را به دست کسانی بسپارید که اطمینان دارید مانند خود شما به‌خوبی از فرزندتان مراقبت می‌کنند. کسی را انتخاب کنید که به او اطمینان دارید و می‌دانید که به خواسته‌های احساسی کودک شما پاسخ می‌دهد، محیطی امن برای او فراهم می‌کند و فرصت یادگیری و رشد ذهنی برای او ایجاد می‌کند.
اگر احساس می‌کنید به کمک نیاز دارید، تقاضای کمک کنید. اگر احساس استرس، اضطراب، افسردگی یا کلافگی دارید یا حس می‌کنید برای مراقبت از کودک خود به کمک نیاز دارید، در این مورد تردید نکنید و حتماً تقاضای کمک کنید. برای این کار می‌توانید به پزشک، مشاوره، اعضای خانواده خود یا یک کانون فرهنگی مراجعه کنید.

The importance of iron for babies
The body needs iron to make hemoglobin, an iron-containing protein found in red blood cells (RBCs). Hemoglobin helps in transporting oxygen and delivering it to all the cells of the body in the blood. If there is no hemoglobin, the body also stops producing healthy red blood cells. Without enough iron, the muscles, tissues and cells of the child do not receive the oxygen they need.

Babies who are breastfed have iron reserves in their bodies until 6 months of age, and they usually get enough iron from breast milk. But formula-fed babies generally get enough iron from iron-fortified formula. After the child gets older and begins to eat complementary or more solid foods, these foods may not contain enough iron and thus be at risk of iron-deficiency anemia.

Iron is a mineral whose excessive consumption leads to poisoning. Therefore, all products containing iron should be kept away from babies and children. Vitamin and mineral supplements for infants are often available as liquids, drops, or syrups because infants usually cannot chew or swallow vitamins well. However, the diet of some babies may be such that there is no need to take iron supplements.

Problems caused by iron deficiency
Iron deficiency delays the growth of the child and causes the following complications:

Behavioral and learning problems
seclusion
Delay in motor skills
muscle weakness
Iron is also important for the immune system, so iron deficiency increases the incidence of infections, colds, and flu attacks.

Iron supplements
If the baby is breastfed: Breastmilk contains a small amount of iron, so babies who are exclusively breastfed are at a higher risk of iron deficiency after 4 months of age. It is recommended for this group of babies to take iron drops or syrup in the right amount until they start taking complementary foods at the age of 6 months. Meat puree, iron-enriched breshtok, and legume puree such as lentils, red beans, cowpeas, and black beans are rich sources of iron. After the addition of complementary foods, the baby should be breastfed for at least another 12 months. The pediatrician determines the duration and amount of iron supplementation for babies under one year old.
If breast milk forms a part of the child's daily food: If breast milk forms more than half of the child's daily food and the child does not eat complementary foods containing iron, the method of taking the supplement will be the same as before.
If the baby feeds on formula: It is recommended to give iron-enriched formula (containing 4 to 12 mg of iron) from birth to the end of one year of age.
Premature babies: Because premature babies have less iron reserves, they often need iron supplements in addition to the iron received from formula or breast milk.
How to use iron syrup
Shake the bottle of iron syrup well before consumption so that the iron in the syrup is completely distributed and the child receives enough iron. Do not give iron syrup or drops to the child on an empty stomach. Although the iron in the supplement is usually better absorbed in the fasting state, this will cause the child's heartache. Giving iron syrup to a child may not be as easy as you think. Many babies and young children spit it out because of the bad taste of iron syrup. To avoid spilling the syrup on the clothes and staining it, it is better to take off the baby's clothes first, put the dropper in the bottle in the baby's mouth and hold it to the side of the mouth. Give the syrup in small amounts to your child.

Because iron syrup is usually unpleasant and the baby's mouth has a bad taste after consuming it, be sure to give the baby a tasty food, such as breast milk or formula, or mix the supplement with a sweet food after taking the baby iron drops. After the child is older and approved by the pediatrician, you can start taking iron supplements.

side effects
Iron supplements cause heartburn, changes in stool concentration, and constipation. Iron supplements are better absorbed if taken on an empty stomach. But if the child's stomach does not tolerate iron fasting, it can be given to the child after eating. Excessive absorption of iron can cause serious side effects, so never give your child iron drops or syrup without consulting a pediatrician. Symptoms of iron overdose include:

severe vomiting
diarrhea
Pale or bluish color (cyanosis) of the skin and nails
weakness
Iron overdose poisoning is an emergency.

Recommendations to increase iron absorption
Give your child foods rich in iron and vitamin C together, because vitamin C increases iron absorption. For example, give the child spaghetti with tomato sauce and meat.
Cook food in cast iron pans. The acid in some foods, such as tomatoes, pulls iron out of the cast iron pan.
Prepare sweets or bread for the child using cereals rich in iron, mix the flour of these cereals with wheat flour, which is low in iron.
Crush the baby food thoroughly. New research has shown that babies absorb iron better from very small pieces of meat than from larger pieces.
Cook grains and legumes with garlic and onion. New research has shown that onions and garlic increase the absorption of iron and zinc from grains.

Do babies need vitamins?
In answer to this question, it should be said that there is no need to do this unless recommended by a doctor. Your doctor can easily determine whether your child has enough of certain vitamins through blood tests and other diagnostic tests. Although experts believe that the supplement does not harm the child. As long as the vitamin supplement does not exceed the allowed daily amount, its consumption by the baby does not cause any problem.

However, some children need vitamins. Especially babies who may have health problems that do not allow the body to absorb vitamins effectively. This condition is commonly observed in children who were born prematurely. Sometimes malnourished children or those who are breastfed by mothers who are vegetarians may need some extra vitamins.

All kinds of vitamins for babies

The type of vitamins that are right for your child depends on their specific health problem. If the child does not have any particular problem, a simple multivitamin will be enough. But some babies need to consume more of a certain vitamin; For example, a child who receives plant-based meals may need more vitamins B12 and D, as well as riboflavin and calcium. The most common vitamin supplements for babies and when to use them are:

Vitamin A
Vitamin A increases normal growth and development, tissue and bone repair, skin and eye health, and body's immune reactions. Good sources of this vitamin include milk, cheese, eggs, and yellow and orange vegetables such as carrots, sweet potatoes, and pumpkins.

Vitamin D
Not getting enough vitamin D can cause a bone disorder called osteoporosis. Because infant formula contains vitamin D, infants who drink at least two cups of formula per day do not need additional vitamin D.

Unlike formula, breast milk does not contain enough vitamin D for babies, but this fact is not a problem because it is believed that babies who are exclusively breastfed will get enough vitamin D from sunlight. However, due to the fact that today the effects of excessive exposure to sunlight are known and sunscreen cream is used more, there is a suspicion that exposure to sunlight will not be enough for babies. Doctors recommend that breastfed babies receive vitamin D supplements for the first two months. The need for vitamin D supplements is a controversial issue, especially for babies with fair skin in sunny climates, so you should consult your pediatrician.

DHA and Omega 3
Essential fatty acids are important for brain and eye development. Fortunately, if the mother has these substances in her diet, then the baby will receive enough in his milk. Powdered milk also has these ingredients. Mothers who do not get enough fatty acids may want to consider a supplement to make sure their baby gets enough, especially if the mother is a vegetarian.

Vitamin B
The family of B vitamins, B2, B3, B6, and B12, contribute to metabolism, energy production, and healthy blood circulation and nervous systems. Good sources of this vitamin include meat, chicken, fish, nuts, eggs, milk, cheese, beans, and soybeans.

Vitamin B12
Vitamin B12 is vital for the development of the nervous system and the prevention of anemia. This vitamin is found naturally in fish, meat, eggs, milk and milk products. If you are breastfeeding and do not consume enough animal protein, it is important to regularly consume reliable sources of vitamin B12 (either through supplements or fortified foods) so that your baby's diet also includes adequate amounts of this vitamin.

Vitamin C
It is easy for a child to get enough vitamin C through diet. A child who eats large amounts of oranges, strawberries, kiwi, tomatoes, peppers, and broccoli will probably get more than enough vitamin C naturally.

Fluoride
When the baby's first teeth come out, it is time to use fluoride. Try giving your child fluoridated water, as supplements can lead to permanent stains on teeth. If the child does not get enough fluoride from the drinking water, then you should consult a doctor to get the right supplements.

Vitamin K
Babies who receive milk from healthy mothers will get enough vitamins, except in some cases where vitamin K and D are not enough. Human milk contains small amounts of vitamin K, and the infant's immature digestive system may not be able to produce enough of this vitamin from the body's own stores. For most babies, this vitamin is injected at birth, and most likely the child will no longer need additional vitamin K.

calcium
Calcium helps build strong bones as the child grows. Good sources of calcium include milk, cheese, yogurt, tofu, and calcium-rich orange juice.

How to choose the right multivitamin?

The first thing to consider when preparing a multivitamin is its nutritional composition. The best multivitamins contain all 13 essential vitamins and 14 essential minerals that the body needs. Vitamins A, C and D, calcium and iron are especially important for a growing child.

Avoid multivitamins that contain artificial colors or flavors and sweeteners. Avoid vitamin lozenges, which are often made from sweet corn syrup. Not only is sugar unnecessary and bad for a child's teeth, it sends a confusing message to the child that vitamins are candy. Look for multivitamins made from whole foods instead of synthetic ingredients.

Vitamin D supplements
Most pediatricians recommend that all children be kept out of direct sunlight as much as possible and use sunscreen when outdoors to avoid the long-term risks of sun exposure that may contribute to skin cancer. Sunscreen prevents the skin from producing vitamin D. For this reason, talk to a pediatrician to receive vitamin D supplement drops.

It is currently recommended that all infants and children receive a minimum daily dose of vitamin D after birth.

If the child is exclusively or partially breastfed: he receives the recommended dose of vitamin D supplement daily from the first few days after birth. The use of the supplement should continue until the child receives at least one liter of whole milk daily. Whole milk should not be used before 12 months of age.
If the baby uses formula: all formula has the minimum recommended amount of vitamin D; Therefore, if the child drinks at least 4 cups of powdered milk, there is no need to use vitamin D supplements. Whole milk should not be used before 12 months of age.

Normal baby sleep
newborn baby
A newborn baby does not yet have a correct understanding of day and night. They tend to sleep around the clock; Because their stomachs are small and cannot hold breast milk or formula for a long time, they wake up frequently to feed, it doesn't matter to them whether it's night or day.
Babies should sleep 14 to 17 hours in 24 hours. Some babies may sleep up to 18-19 hours a day.
Babies wake up every two hours. Breastfed babies need to be fed about 8 to 12 times a day. Babies who are fed with powdered milk, the number of times they are fed is less and they need to be fed every 3-4 hours.
Babies who sleep for long periods of time need to wake up to feed. Wake your baby every 3-4 hours so that you can gain the ideal weight, which is usually more important in the first few weeks.
The first month of a baby's life may be the most difficult period for parents, because the child tends to wake up and feed several times at night.
It may take a few weeks for the baby's brain to understand the difference between night and day. Unfortunately, there is no trick to speeding up this process, feeding and changing your baby's diapers can help you get a good night's sleep.
In order for the baby to sleep more at night, do not try to keep him awake during the day. Babies who are overtired are more prone to insomnia than babies who have had enough sleep during the day.
If your newborn is crying, it's okay to rock, hold, or sing to soothe him. In the first months of a baby's life, restlessness is common. (In fact, babies who are properly cared for during the day are less likely to be bloated and fussy).
Babies one to three months old
When you think that sleeping more has become a distant dream for you, the baby sleeps longer during the night. Little by little, the baby's sleep cycle becomes closer to the parent's sleep cycle, and his need for night feeding may decrease. At this stage, the duration of night sleep is considered 5 to 6 hours. Since babies at this age are more awake and alert and are more aware of their surroundings during the day, they are more likely to get tired and fall asleep during the night. But still, the normal range of baby sleep can be very different.

Babies up to 3 months should sleep 14-17 hours in 24 hours. Many of them will nap two or three times during the day and will sleep longer at night after night feeding.

If the parents rock the baby to sleep before going to sleep, he may get used to rocking again after waking up at night. Instead, try when the baby is drowsy but still awake; Put him in a baby bed or crib, so that he gradually learns to fall asleep on his own.
Some babies are a little tossed, fussy, or even cry before they fall asleep. Unless you think your baby is sick or hungry, leave him alone for a few minutes to see what happens; He will probably calm down.
If the baby wakes up during the period you want him to sleep, minimize the activities around him. Try to lower the light intensity of the lights; Resist the temptation to play and talk with the child. Feed your baby and change his diaper, then transfer him to his bed.
If your baby wakes up early for morning feedings, some small changes to her schedule may be okay. Parents can wake up the child late at night and feed him if their sleep schedule allows.
Babies 4 to 7 months old
At this age, the baby's sleep pattern should gradually become regular. Some babies, especially those who are breastfed, may wake up at night. But in most cases, there will be no need to feed in the middle of the night. Most babies at this age sleep 12 to 15 hours, which includes a long period of nighttime sleep and at least two daily naps. In this stage, the average sleep duration is about 3 to 4 hours. By 6 months, most babies sleep for 9 hours or more at night, while they may wake up in short bursts.

Calming activities can calm the baby and send him to bed. A warm bath after reading a story or singing a lullaby can evoke the end of the day for the child. These similar activities can be used for several years.
Parents want their baby to fall asleep on their own. This may involve making regular nighttime routines and putting the baby to bed (when the baby is sleepy but awake). If the baby cries, move away from him for a few minutes. The child may become quiet and fall asleep.
If the baby continues to cry, briefly return to the baby and soothe the baby without picking him up and holding him. It may be necessary to repeat this several times, so that the baby learns that crying does not work. This may be difficult for parents, because hearing the baby cry is distracting for parents. If you are sure that the baby is okay, leave him alone for a while to calm down.
Children who sleep through the night may sometimes wake up near the morning. Allow some time for the baby to go back to sleep on its own. Even if the baby is restless, give him a few minutes. After making sure the baby is okay, leave the baby alone to sleep. Remember that any rocking, feeding or talking to the baby may cause the baby to wake up every night for your attention.
Separation anxiety is a natural stage of the baby's development process that may wake up six-month-old to one-year-old babies crying in the middle of the night. If this happens, give him some time to calm down, just like when the baby wakes up at other times. If necessary, without taking the baby out of bed, try to calm him down for a short time.
Babies 6 to 12 months old
A baby's sleep disorder is common in the second half of the first year of his life. Some babies may wake up in the middle of the night with loud noises or cries, then calm down when mom or dad comes into the room. This is due to separation anxiety, a normal stage of the baby's development process that occurs during this period. If this happens, give him some time to calm down, just like when the baby wakes up at other times. If necessary, try to calm the baby for a short time without taking him out of bed. Most babies this age should sleep 12 to 15 hours a day, which includes 9 to 12 hours of sleep at night. The baby may still nap twice a day. Some babies fall asleep in 30 minutes, while others fall asleep in up to 2 hours.

During the period when the risk of SIDS (sudden infant death syndrome) is high, for the first year or at least six months, keep the baby in your room without sharing a bed with the baby.
