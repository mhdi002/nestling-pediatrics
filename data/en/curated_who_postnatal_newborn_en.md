# Postnatal care and newborn danger signs

Summarised from *Pregnancy, Childbirth, Postpartum and Newborn Care: a guide
for essential practice, 3rd edition* (WHO, UNFPA, UNICEF, World Bank, 2015).
Written for parents; the source is a clinical guide for health workers, and
nothing here replaces assessment by a midwife or clinician.

## Routine postnatal check-ups
WHO's recommended schedule of postnatal contacts after birth:

- **first contact:** within 24 hours of childbirth
- **second contact:** on day 3 (48–72 hours)
- **third contact:** between day 7 and day 14 after birth
- **final contact:** a clinic visit at 6 weeks after birth

These visits check the mother's recovery and the baby's feeding, weight and
wellbeing, and are worth keeping even when everything seems to be going well.
Bringing a partner or family member to at least one visit is encouraged.

After an uncomplicated vaginal birth in a health facility, healthy mothers and
newborns should receive care in the facility for at least 24 hours after birth.

## Maternal danger signs — go to hospital immediately
Go to a hospital or health centre **immediately, day or night, without
waiting**, for any of these after birth:

- vaginal bleeding that soaks more than 2–3 pads in 20–30 minutes, or bleeding
  that increases rather than decreases
- convulsions (fits)
- headache with blurred vision
- fast or difficult breathing
- fever together with being too weak to get out of bed
- severe abdominal pain
- calf pain, redness or swelling, shortness of breath, or chest pain

## Maternal signs needing care as soon as possible
Go to a health centre promptly, though not as an emergency, for:

- fever
- abdominal pain
- generally feeling unwell
- breasts that are swollen, red or tender, or a sore nipple
- dribbling urine, or pain passing urine
- pain in the perineum, or discharge of pus
- foul-smelling vaginal discharge (lochia)

## Follow-up timing after a treated problem
If a problem has already been identified and treated, the guide sets expected
review intervals — useful for knowing when to go back:

- fever, urinary tract infection, or perineal infection or pain: 2 days
- high blood pressure, or urinary incontinence: 1 week
- severe anaemia, postpartum low mood, or HIV infection: 2 weeks
- moderate anaemia: 4 weeks
- after hospital treatment for any complication: as instructed, and no later
  than 2 weeks

## Postpartum mood
Low mood in the days after birth is common and usually settles. It is worth
raising at a postnatal contact rather than waiting, and the guide sets a
2-week review for postpartum blues. Seek help sooner if a mother cannot care
for herself or the baby, feels hopeless, or has thoughts of harming herself or
the baby — that is an emergency.

## Caring for a newborn at home
Core practices in the first weeks:

- keep the baby warm and in skin-to-skin contact, which supports temperature,
  breathing and breastfeeding
- breastfeed early and on demand, day and night
- keep the cord stump clean and dry, and leave it uncovered; do not apply
  substances to it
- delay the first bath, and keep the baby covered and warm afterwards
- wash hands before handling the baby
- keep the baby away from anyone with an infection, and away from smoke
- attend the scheduled postnatal contacts and immunization visits

Seek urgent care for a newborn who is feeding poorly or refusing to feed, is
breathing fast or with difficulty, has a fever or feels cold, is unusually
sleepy or hard to wake, has convulsions, has yellow palms and soles, or has a
red, swollen or discharging umbilicus.
