# Development milestones for 2 to 5 year olds (English, curated from AAP public guidance)

Parent-facing developmental milestones for the toddler and preschool years,
continuing where the infant milestone notes in this library stop. Children vary
widely and missing a single skill is not by itself a delay — but do raise concerns
early rather than waiting, because early support works best. General information
only, not a developmental assessment.

## Development and milestones at 2 years

Around the second birthday the AAP describes a child who walks alone, pulls toys
behind them while walking, carries a large toy or several toys while walking, begins
to run, stands on tiptoe, kicks a ball, and climbs onto and down from furniture
unassisted. With their hands they scribble spontaneously, turn a container over to
pour out the contents, and build a tower of four blocks or more; a hand preference
may start to show. For language, a two year old points to an object or picture when
it is named, recognises the names of familiar people, objects and body parts, and
uses two- to four-word sentences. Thinking becomes more organised: they find objects
hidden under two or three covers, begin to sort by shape and colour, begin
make-believe play, string activities together into a logical sequence rather than
playing randomly, match similar shapes in sorting toys and simple jigsaws, and start
solving small problems in their head instead of only by trial and error with their
hands. Socially they imitate adults and older children, become increasingly aware of
themselves as separate from other people, and show growing independence.

Source: AAP HealthyChildren, *Developmental Milestones: 2 Year Olds* and *Cognitive Development: Two-Year-Old*.

## Development and milestones at 3 to 4 years

Between 3 and 4 years the AAP describes a child who hops and stands on one foot for
up to 5 seconds, goes up and down stairs without support, kicks and throws a ball,
catches a bounced ball, pedals a tricycle and climbs well. Their hands can copy a
square, draw a person with a head and one other body part, use child-safe scissors,
draw circles and squares, and begin copying capital letters. In language they
understand the ideas of "same" and "different" and simple prepositions such as "on"
and "under", speak in three-word sentences, are understood by people outside the
family, and tell stories. In thinking they name some colours correctly, understand
counting and may know a few numbers, follow three-part commands, recall parts of a
story and play imaginatively. Socially they are interested in new experiences,
cooperate with other children and share, dress themselves, work out conflicts, eat
independently and manage the toilet themselves. Emotionally, many imagine that
unfamiliar images are "monsters" and often cannot yet separate fantasy from reality,
which is why fears of the dark are so common at this age.

Source: AAP HealthyChildren, *Developmental Milestones: 3 to 4 Year Olds*.

## Development and milestones at 4 to 5 years

Between 4 and 5 years the AAP describes a child who stands on one foot for 10
seconds or longer, climbs stairs alternating feet without support, hops, does
somersaults, swings and climbs. Fine motor skills sharpen: they copy a triangle and
other geometric patterns, draw a person with at least three body parts, print some
letters, dress and undress without help, do up and undo medium-sized buttons, use a
fork and spoon and usually manage their own toilet needs. In language they recall
part of a story, use four-word sentences, use words that are completely
understandable to strangers, use the future tense, tell longer stories and say their
own name and address. In thinking they count 10 or more objects, name at least four
colours correctly, understand time better, and know about everyday household things.
Socially they want to please and be like their friends, are more likely to agree to
rules, engage in well-developed imaginative play, brush their own teeth and show
more independence overall.

Source: AAP HealthyChildren, *Developmental Milestones: 4 to 5 Year Olds*.

## When to raise a development concern about a 2 to 5 year old

Milestones are a prompt for a conversation, not a test to pass. The AAP suggests
alerting your paediatrician if a child cannot walk by eighteen months, does not
speak at least fifteen words by eighteen months, does not use two-word sentences by
age two, or does not follow simple instructions by age two. For a preschooler, the
AAP's developmental health watch lists reasons to check in with your clinician
including extreme fearfulness or aggression, difficulty separating from parents,
poor concentration, little interaction with other children, only superficial
responses to other people, very little imaginative play, persistent sadness or
passivity, difficulty understanding instructions with more than one part, and
trouble with basic self-care such as dressing, sleeping or washing hands. Losing a
skill your child previously had is always worth reporting, whatever their age. Ask
your clinician about a developmental screening rather than waiting to see whether a
concern resolves on its own.

Source: AAP HealthyChildren, *Developmental Milestones: 2 Year Olds* and *Developmental Milestones: 4 to 5 Year Olds*.
