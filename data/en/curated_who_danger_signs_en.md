# WHO/IMCI danger signs and when to seek care

Parent-facing summaries of WHO Integrated Management of Childhood Illness
(IMCI) guidance. These describe when to seek care. They are not a diagnosis
and never replace assessment by a health worker.

## General danger signs in a sick child
WHO IMCI treats four findings as general danger signs in a child aged 2 months
to 5 years, and any one of them means the child needs urgent assessment at a
health facility:

- unable to drink or breastfeed
- vomits everything
- has had convulsions (fits) with this illness
- is lethargic or unconscious — unusually sleepy, difficult to wake, or not
  responding normally

A child with any general danger sign should be taken for emergency care
without waiting to see whether other symptoms improve. Do not delay to give
food or fluids first if the child cannot swallow.

## Danger signs in a young infant under 2 months
Newborns and small infants deteriorate quickly and show fewer specific signs.
Seek urgent care if a baby under 2 months has any of:

- not feeding well or refusing to feed
- convulsions
- fast breathing (60 breaths per minute or more)
- severe chest indrawing — the lower chest wall pulls in sharply on breathing in
- fever (37.5 °C / 99.5 °F or higher) or low body temperature
  (below 35.5 °C / 95.9 °F)
- movement only when stimulated, or no movement at all
- yellow palms and soles (jaundice reaching the hands and feet)
- a red or draining umbilicus, or skin pustules

Any fever in an infant under 3 months warrants same-day medical assessment,
even if the baby otherwise looks well.

## Fast breathing thresholds by age
WHO defines fast breathing by age. Count breaths for a full 60 seconds while
the child is calm and not crying:

- under 2 months: 60 breaths per minute or more
- 2 to 11 months: 50 breaths per minute or more
- 12 months to 5 years: 40 breaths per minute or more

Fast breathing with cough or difficult breathing suggests pneumonia and needs
prompt assessment. Chest indrawing, grunting, blue lips or tongue, or inability
to drink indicate severe disease and need emergency care.

## Signs of dehydration
Assess a child with diarrhoea or vomiting for dehydration. Seek urgent care if
you see:

- sunken eyes, and in infants a sunken soft spot (fontanelle)
- skin that returns slowly when gently pinched
- restlessness and irritability, or lethargy
- drinking eagerly and appearing very thirsty, or being unable to drink
- markedly reduced urine — for example no wet nappy for 6 hours or more
- no tears when crying

Severe dehydration is a medical emergency requiring immediate treatment.

## When to seek care for fever
Fever is a symptom, not a diagnosis, and its cause matters more than the
number. Seek medical assessment when:

- the child is under 3 months and has any fever
- fever lasts more than 3 days
- fever occurs with a stiff neck, a rash that does not fade when pressed,
  persistent vomiting, or a convulsion
- the child is very drowsy, difficult to wake, or unusually irritable
- there are breathing difficulties

Focus on how the child looks and behaves rather than the temperature alone. A
child who is alert, drinking and playing between fever peaks is usually less
concerning than a child who is listless when the fever comes down.
