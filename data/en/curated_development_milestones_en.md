# Early childhood development milestones (English, curated from CDC/WHO public materials)

Development varies widely. These ranges describe skills many children achieve; missing one skill is not always delay — discuss concerns with your clinician. Based on CDC Learn the Signs Act Early themes and WHO child development concepts.

## Social and emotional 0–6 months

Newborns seek closeness: calming when held, brief eye contact, and by 2–3 months social smiling in response to faces and voices. They begin cooing vowel sounds and turn toward familiar voices. By 4–6 months many laugh, enjoy mirror play, and show interest in other babies. Separation anxiety typically increases later — not expected in early months. Red flags for urgent discussion: no social smile by 3 months, no eye contact, extreme stiffness or floppiness, or loss of skills once gained.

## Motor development 0–6 months

Tummy time builds head control: many infants hold head steady when upright by 3–4 months and roll one way by 4–6 months. They bring hands to mouth and reach for toys. Sitting with support appears around 4–6 months; independent sitting often later. Always supervise on elevated surfaces once rolling begins. Premature infants use corrected age for milestone comparison — ask your clinician. Seek evaluation if marked asymmetry (uses only one side), no reaching by 6 months, or very delayed head control.

## Communication 0–6 months — not words yet

Expect cooing, gurgling, and turning to sound — not recognizable words at 3–6 months. Babbling with consonants (ba, da) often emerges around 6–9 months. Respond to baby's sounds to encourage back-and-forth "conversation." Reading and singing support language exposure. Hearing screening is routine newborn care — follow up if refer. Concerns: no vocalization by 4 months, not orienting to loud sounds, or regression in babbling.

## Motor and play 6–12 months

Many infants sit without support, transfer objects hand to hand, bang toys, and crawl or scoot by 9–10 months. Pulling to stand and cruising along furniture may appear near 9–12 months. Fine motor: pincer grasp for small foods around 9 months. Offer safe exploration and varied textures. Childproof home as mobility increases. Discuss if no sitting by 9 months, no bearing weight on legs with support, or only one-sided movement.

## Walking 9–18 months

Independent walking timing varies widely. Many toddlers take first independent steps between about 9 and 15 months; cruising and walking with hand-hold often come first. Around 13–14 months it is common not to walk alone yet if the child is pulling to stand, cruising, or taking steps with support — that can still be within a typical range. Encourage safe floor play, standing practice, and barefoot time indoors when safe. Seek pediatric advice sooner if there is little weight-bearing on the legs, marked asymmetry, loss of motor skills, very low muscle tone, or you remain worried; many clinicians discuss evaluation if walking has not begun by about 18 months (earlier when other red flags are present). Premature infants often use corrected age for milestone comparison — ask your clinician.

## Communication 9–18 months — first words

Babbling expands; first words often near 12 months (mama/dada as labels, not only sounds). By 18 months many use several words and follow simple commands ("give me the ball"). Pointing to show interest (joint attention) is an important social-communication milestone. Limit passive screen time; interactive talk matters more. Seek hearing/speech evaluation if no words by 18 months, loss of words, or no pointing/gestures by 12 months — earlier if you remain worried.

## Toddler development 18–36 months

Running, climbing, and scribbling emerge. Two-word phrases often appear around 2 years; strangers understand some speech by 3 years though articulation is still immature. Parallel play shifts toward cooperative play with peers. Toilet training readiness varies — look for dry periods, interest, and ability to communicate needs. Behavior tantrums are common as autonomy grows. Use developmental screening tools (ASQ) at well visits. Autism screening (M-CHAT-R) is recommended around 18 and 24 months in many systems — follow positive screens with clinician referral, not online diagnosis.
