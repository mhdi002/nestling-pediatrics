- (info_0088) ## Iron
Iron is required for hemoglobin and oxygen delivery. Exclusively breastfed infants often need iron supplementation after about 4 months until iron-rich complementary foods are established. Formula-fed infants usually receive iron-fortified formula. Premature infants have lower iron stores and commonly need supplements. Excess iron is toxic — keep supplements away from children and follow clinician dosing.
- (info_0052) Recommendations to increase iron absorption
Give your child foods rich in iron and vitamin C together, because vitamin C increases iron absorption. For example, give the child spaghetti with tomato sauce and meat.
Cook food in cast iron pans. The acid in some foods, such as tomatoes, pulls iron out of the cast iron pan.
Prepare sweets or bread for the child using cereals rich in iron, mix the flour of these cereals with wheat flour, which is low in iron.
Crush the baby food thoroughly. New research has shown that babies absorb iron better from very small pieces of meat than from larger pieces.
Cook grains and legumes with garlic and onion. New research has shown that onions and garlic increase the absorption of iron and zinc from grains.
- (info_0048) Iron supplements
If the baby is breastfed: Breastmilk contains a small amount of iron, so babies who are exclusively breastfed are at a higher risk of iron deficiency after 4 months of age. It is recommended for this group of babies to take iron drops or syrup in the right amount until they start taking complementary foods at the age of 6 months. Meat puree, iron-enriched breshtok, and legume puree such as lentils, red beans, cowpeas, and black beans are rich sources of iron. After the addition of complementary foods, the baby should be breastfed for at least another 12 months. The pediatrician determines the duration and amount of iron supplementation for babies under one year old.
If breast milk forms a part of the child's daily food: If breast milk forms more than half of the child's daily food and the child does not eat complementary foods containing iron, the method of taking the supplement will be the same as before.
If the baby feeds on formula: It is recommended to give iron-enriched formula (containing 4 to 12 mg of iron) from birth to the end of one year of age.
Premature babies: Because premature babies have less iron reserves, they often need iron supplements in addition to the iron received from formula or breast milk.
How to use iron syrup
Shake the bottle of iron syrup well before consumption so that the iron in the syrup is completely distributed and the child receives enough iron. Do not give iron syrup or drops to the child on an empty stomach. Although the iron in the supplement is usually better absorbed in the fasting state, this will cause the child's heartache. Giving iron syrup to a child may not be as easy as you think. Many babies and young children spit it out because of the bad taste of iron syrup. To avoid spilling the syrup on the clothes and staining it, it is better to take off the baby's clothes first, put the dropper in the bottle in the baby's mouth and hold it to the side of the mouth. Give the syrup in small amounts to your child.
For diagnosis or treatment decisions, consult a pediatric clinician.
